# M51.9 — Mid-phase adversarial review

**Depends on:** [M46](m46.md)–[M51](m51.md).
**Discharges:** nothing on the scope tables. It is the first of the phase's two
reviews, and [M32.9](m32.9.md) is the specification it runs to — method,
dimensions, adversarial verification and the rule that only confirmed findings
are reported. None of that is restated here.

## Why here

Everything numerically below this row is either **the interface** or **the one
identity change that a person has to be able to complete alone**. Everything
above it changes tenancy, credentials, erasure or the deployment shape. Those
are different kinds of risk and reviewing them together would let the second kind
set the tone for the first.

Concretely, this review sees the redesign ([M46](m46.md)–[M48](m48.md)) while it
is still the thing later milestones will build inside, rather than after five
milestones have already built on top of it. A redesign reviewed at the end of a
phase is a redesign nobody can act on.

## Risks

A review of a redesign has a failure mode a code review does not: the reviewer
adapts. Six milestones of working inside the new interface makes its worst
decisions feel inevitable, and the person best placed to review it is the person
least able to see it fresh. That is why the blind tasks are re-run rather than
inspected, and why the reviewer is not the only input.

The second risk is scheduling. This review sits between the interface work and
the identity work, which means findings here land on the surface every later
milestone builds against. That is the point, and it is also why a finding
confirmed here is expensive: it is cheapest to fix now and most expensive at
[M57.9](m57.9.md).
