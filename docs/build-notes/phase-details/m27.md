# M27 — Organizations: invitations and joining

**Depends on:** [M21](m21.md) [M22](m22.md) [M25](m25.md) [M26](m26.md).
**Discharges:** Collaboration scope row *Organizations: sharing, invites, team
management* — invites half; [M28](m28.md) completes it.

An owner or admin invites a person into their organization by revocable
single-use token. Redeeming it produces a colleague, not a tenant.

## Done means

- A new **live** `invitations` table exists — typed columns, not a 00600 dormant
  table. Token stored hashed like session tokens, with a TTL, single-use, and
  revocable.
- Invite create / list / revoke are gated by `members.write`, its first
  enforcement, with the API-key delegability decision recorded and tested.
- Delivery: emailed when [M26](m26.md)'s mailer is configured; a copyable invite
  link exists either way. Both paths tested.
- **Redemption creates membership only** (decision D6): the invited user joins
  the inviter's organization and does *not* receive an auto-provisioned personal
  org or workspace. The "one personal org per user" invariant is deliberately
  broken here, and the signup-form label differs per path accordingly.
- The invited account remains capable of owning an organization later **without
  needing a second account** — it is an ordinary account, and any role or
  permission can be granted to it by the ordinary path. This is the requirement
  D6 attached to membership-only. The `orgs.create` permission itself is
  [M28](m28.md)'s to build, with its own seed migration and delegability
  decision (decision D16); M27 owes the *capability*, not the permission, and
  proves it by granting an invited account an owner role and checking it then
  holds an owner-only permission.
  *(Amended 2026-08-01 at step 3.4 — the bullet said the permission "exists",
  present tense, and it does not. See decisions.md.)*
- **With `LINKCTRL_SIGNUP_MODE=closed`, no invite creates a new account**
  (decision D7): under `closed`, invites may only add users who already exist.
  The environment ceiling stays absolute, matching the recorded rule that no
  session can open a closed instance. With mode `invite` or above, a new
  registrant is admitted through the invite into the inviter's org, and
  configuration.md's "invite behaves as closed" sentence is deleted because it is
  no longer true.
- Redemption and its error paths reveal nothing about whether an email has an
  account — no enumeration oracle, matching the login rule.
- Invite issued / revoked / redeemed are audit events ([M21](m21.md)); an accepted
  invite notifies the inviter in-app ([M22](m22.md)).

## Reopened 2026-08-01 — a scoped key mints an unscoped account

M27 shipped and a claim it is cited for turned out false. Reopened by the owner
on [M32.9](m32.9.md)'s triage.

**What is false.** `docs/SECURITY.md:45` says *"The role an invitation may carry
is capped at its issuer's own rank, **which is why `members.write` is safe to
delegate to an API key**."* The cap holds and the conclusion does not. An API
key scoped to `members.write` alone can `POST /api/v1/invitations` — asserted on
purpose by `TestMembersWriteIsDelegableToAnAPIKey` — read the raw redemption URL
out of its own 201 response, and redeem it into a new interactive account at up
to the key owner's rank. The account is not a key, so `requireSessionActor` no
longer refuses it, and every scope `auth.NonDelegableScopes` exists to withhold
becomes reachable. Revoking the key does not revoke the account. Recorded as
[F29](../deferred-findings.md).

**There is no benign case.** `members.write` is granted only to owner and admin
(`00700_seed.sql:53-60`), so every key that can legally hold it escalates to at
least admin — which includes `apikeys.write` and `audit.read`. `SIGNUP_MODE`
defaults to `closed`, which bounds it, but `docs/SECURITY.md:238` recommends
`closed` **or `invite`** as a safe posture and `invite` is the vulnerable mode;
`closed` is also weaker than it reads, because redemption under `closed` still
succeeds for an address that already has an account.

### Done means, for the reopening

- **This is a decision before it is a fix, and the decision is recorded first.**
  D28 concluded `members.write` is delegable by reasoning on the *rank* axis;
  `NonDelegableScopes` governs the *credential-type* axis, and the two were
  never reconciled. D18's limbs — escalating, disclosing — are the test to apply,
  and F12 already records that those limbs do not cover the map they govern.
- **The candidate answers, with their costs, go to the owner before code.**
  Moving `members.write` into `NonDelegableScopes` breaks a shipped,
  deliberately-tested capability and one integration test asserts the opposite.
  Bounding what a key-issued invitation may carry — a rank ceiling below the
  issuer, or refusing account *creation* from a key-issued invitation — keeps
  the capability and changes the invitation contract. Neither is a correction.
- **Whatever lands, D28 is corrected rather than left standing**, and
  `docs/SECURITY.md:45`'s sentence goes with it. It is the sentence a reader
  trusts when deciding what to put on a key.
- **A test that fails on the whole chain**, not on one link: mint a key with
  `members.write`, invite at the owner's rank, redeem, and assert the resulting
  principal cannot reach a non-delegable scope.

## Risks

The closed-mode rule needs its own test matrix: existing-user-under-closed
succeeds, new-user-under-closed is refused, and neither leaks whether the address
was already registered.
